What's happening behind the scenes

The model

Each item i lives at a 2D point zi on the unit disc — these come from an autoencoder over past behaviour. The customer's taste is another vector zu in the same space. The probability that this customer clicks on item i, when shown, is the (clipped) inner product:

click model

After each round we have a binary observation yt ∈ {0, 1}. We update our point estimate of the customer's taste with ridge regression on the observations so far:

ridge update

To pick the next item, the algorithm uses LinUCB: among all items, it shows the one whose upper plausible click probability is highest. The bonus term grows with our uncertainty in that direction, so under-explored corners of the embedding pull the algorithm toward them.

LinUCB action selection

Mode A — Learn this customer

Press Play and watch the solid blue estimate arrow rotate toward the (hidden) truth. The translucent ellipse around the arrow is our 95% confidence region — it shrinks as evidence builds. Toggle Show truth to compare against the dashed truth arrow.

Mode B — The cold-start cliff

Press New customer. The estimate resets to zero; LinUCB must explore from scratch. The first 5–10 rounds look almost random — this is the cold-start tax. With representations from past customers, you'd already start much closer to the truth.
