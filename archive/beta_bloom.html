The trick. Instead of asking "which arm has the highest mean?", Thompson sampling asks "which arm is most likely the best?". Each round we draw one sample from each posterior — those wobbly darts you see — and pull the arm whose sample is largest. Wide curves yield wild darts (free exploration); narrow curves yield confident darts (automatic exploitation). One algorithm, no hand-tuned ε.

What to look for. Early on, all three Beta(1,1) curves are flat — every arm gets sampled. Within ~30 rounds the worst arm's curve has slid left and gone narrow; within ~150 the best arm dominates. Cumulative regret grows fast at first, then flattens — exactly the sub-linear curve theory predicts.
