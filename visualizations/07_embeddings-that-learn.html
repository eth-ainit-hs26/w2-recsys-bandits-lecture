Embeddings that learn

What to watch
  • The 16 books reorganize into four distinct corners — the same quadrants you saw in the latent-axes lab, but rotated/reflected at random.
  • The 21 readers collapse to two clusters (light vs demanding). The matrix doesn't contain enough fiction/nonfiction signal in reader preferences to separate A1 from A3 — they liked the same things.
  • Alice (id 1) and Tessa (id 20) have bit-identical rating rows. They must land on the same point.
  • Re-seed: same geometry, different rotation. The axes have no fixed meaning.

What's happening?

We minimise by full-batch gradient descent over the visible entries only — the cells where a reader actually said ✅ or ❌. Missing (➖) and ambiguous (?) cells are masked out and never enter the loss.

Each step computes the gradient of the squared error at every visible cell, then nudges every reader vector $u_i$ and book vector $v_j$ slightly down-hill. The four corners aren't put there — they emerge.

Press New seed after convergence: the four-corner geometry reappears, but rotated or reflected. Latent factors are not unique: any rotation of $U,V$ leaves $UV^\top$ unchanged. This is why the axes have no fixed meaning until we name them.

Alice ≡ Tessa. Reader 1 (Alice) and reader 20 (Tessa) rate every book identically. From the matrix's point of view they are the same person, so the optimizer has no information to separate them — they must converge to the same 2D point.
