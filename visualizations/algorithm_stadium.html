Cumulative regret

Regret is what you couldn't see — the reward you'd have gotten always pulling the best urn, minus what you actually got. A flat slope means the algorithm has stopped paying tuition. A steep slope means it's still bleeding. Pure random and ε = 0.50 grow linearly. Naive estimate-then-exploit and ε = 0.05 grow linearly *if* they commit to the wrong urn. ε = 0.20 typically wins.

What each algorithm is doing

Arm-pull bars show the fraction of pulls each algorithm has spent on each urn. Naive locks onto whichever urn looked best after its 10 exploration rounds. ε-greedy keeps a steady tax on losers — the size of the tax is exactly ε. Pure random taxes everyone equally.

Cumulative regret

The punchline. ε too small (or zero, as in naive estimate-then-exploit) means: if you commit to the wrong urn, regret grows linearly forever. ε too large means: you keep paying full exploration cost, regret again grows linearly. A middling ε — here 0.20 — produces sublinear regret on average. The take-away: the explore/exploit dial is real, and tuning it matters.

Tip: hit "Reseed everything" a few times. On some seeds the naive learner gets lucky and locks onto the best urn — its regret then flattens completely. On other seeds it locks onto a near-best urn and you can see the linear-regret slope. ε = 0.20 stays sublinear across almost all seeds.
