Week 2 · Recommendation Systems

Eleven ways to see a matrix

A lecture-companion series of interactive visualizations for matrix factorization and recommendation systems. Each piece teaches one idea from w2_recsys.pdf — drag, slide, and watch the math move.

Motivation

— slides 2–3

A messy matrix of preferences. Reorder rows and columns by hand and the structure jumps out — but how do we automate the discovery?

01🎯

Block-diagonal puzzle

Drag rows and columns until the hidden cluster structure clicks into place — and the score celebrates.

Latent geometry

— slides 6–19

Items and users live in the same Euclidean space. The inner product of their vectors is the predicted preference.

02🧪

Latent-axes laboratory

Swap the labels on the X and Y axes and watch the same items rearrange. Punchline: the geometry is real, the names are not.
03🧭

Inner-product compass

Drag a user's taste vector. Half the plane glows green, half glows red. Recommendations re-rank in real time.
04📊

Dot-product bistro

Bars for the user, bars for the item, products in between, and one number at the bottom: the entire formula, breathing.

Forward model · matrix multiplication

— slides 21–25

A user-embedding matrix times an item-embedding matrix produces the full ratings matrix. Each cell is a dot product; the whole thing is a sum of rank-1 outer products.

05⚡

Build a single rating

Click any empty cell. Its row and column light up, products fly out, and the answer lands. "Compute all" cascades the matrix.
06🎨

Outer-product painters

Add rank-1 layers one at a time and watch the matrix snap into focus. Same trick compresses an image — proof on the second tab.

Learning the embeddings

— slides 26–28

Reverse the question. Given just the matrix, recover the user and item vectors. Gradient descent, with no axis labels, finds the four-corner geometry on its own.

07🌱

Embeddings that learn · climax

Random dots → four-corner cluster geometry, live. Re-seed and the same structure re-emerges in a different rotation.
08🧩

Filling the holes

The practical use of factorization: predict the unseen cells. Watch validation accuracy climb as the model trains.

Real data · interpolation

— payoff

Embeddings as personality vectors, applied to a curated movie set and to interpolation between two users.

09🗺️

MovieLens taste map

50 movies in a 2D taste plane. Drop a "you" vector and watch top-10 recommendations morph as you drag.
10↔️

Embedding interpolation

Slide between Michael and Fabian. Their predicted preferences morph cell-by-cell along the way.

Failure modes · bridge to bandits

— next half of W2

Factorization needs ratings to learn. New users have none — and the prediction quality drops off a cliff. Hence: exploration. Hence: bandits.

11📉

Cold-start cliff

Slide N from 0 to 16 ratings for a new user. Watch the prediction quality climb — and see why we need to ask the right questions first.
