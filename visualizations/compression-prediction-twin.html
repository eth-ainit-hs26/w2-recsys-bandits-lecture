The compression-prediction twin

A perfect predictor is a perfect compressor. The forecaster outputs a probability for each next symbol; an arithmetic coder uses those exact probabilities to write bits. Watch the forecaster's per-symbol cross-entropy and the encoder's per-symbol bit rate track each other exactly — both bounded below by Shannon's entropy H.

Why are these numbers literally equal?

The forecaster outputs p̂ over the alphabet. The arithmetic coder uses p̂ to allocate −log₂ p̂(x) bits to each actual symbol x. That is also exactly the cross-entropy term for that step. Sum, divide by n: same number on both sides, by construction.

Both quantities converge to Shannon's entropy H(p) from above as the forecaster learns. The excess above H is the Kullback–Leibler divergence KL(p ‖ p̂) — the price of imperfect prediction, paid in bits.
