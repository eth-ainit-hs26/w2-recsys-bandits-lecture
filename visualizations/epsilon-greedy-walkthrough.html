ε-greedy: a step-by-step walkthrough

One click per micro-step. Watch the roulette decide whether to exploit (probability 1−ε) or explore (probability ε), then a ball is pulled from the chosen urn and the corresponding estimate p̂k is updated. The three urns have hidden biases p1 > p2 > p3; the orange-vs-white mix inside each urn is the truth the algorithm is trying to discover.

round 0  ·  phase init
Set ε with the slider, then press Next to spin the roulette.

Algorithm

  1. Initialise. For each urn k = 1, 2, 3, set the observation list to empty. Choose ε ∈ (0, 1).
  2. For round t = 1, 2, 3, …
  3. Sample u ~ Uniform[0, 1].
  4. If u < ε  [ explore ]: pick urn k uniformly at random.
  5. Else  [ exploit ]: pick urn k = arg maxi p̂i.
  6. Pull a ball from urn k. Observe x ∈ {0, 1}.
  7. Append x to urn k's observation list. Recompute p̂k = mean of that list.

What you are watching

The cyan slice of the roulette has angular size ε·360°; the violet slice fills the rest. The pointer on the rim shows where a uniform draw u ∈ [0, 1] falls; that draw decides whether the round is an exploration step (cyan) or an exploitation step (violet).

Under exploitation, the algorithm picks the urn with the highest current empirical mean p̂i. Under exploration, all three urns are equally likely.

Try ε = 0.05 vs ε = 0.50 across many rounds. Small ε commits to whichever urn looked best early; large ε keeps wasting pulls on losers. The decaying-ε option (ε ← ε·0.95 per round) lowers the explore tax over time, a common fix.
