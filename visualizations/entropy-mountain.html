Why the bell curve is a floor, not a hill

Shannon (1948) proved that no compression scheme can describe an urn's draws in fewer than H(p) bits per ball, on average. At p = 0.5 you need a full bit per draw — every flip is genuinely surprising. At p = 0.99 the urn is almost all blue, and you need only ≈ 0.08 bits per draw: long runs of blues compress away.

Entropy will reappear next as the information gain in a Bayesian bandit: the more uncertain the arm (the closer its posterior is to the top of this mountain), the more we learn from pulling it. The mountain is the budget for curiosity.
