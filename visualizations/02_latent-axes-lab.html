Where do these axes come from?

The four axes — Fiction / Nonfiction × Light / Demanding — were not invented for this lecture. Fiction vs. Nonfiction is the oldest taxonomic axis in libraries (it predates the Dewey Decimal System). Light vs. Demanding maps directly onto readability indices like Flesch reading ease and the Lexile framework that schools and publishers have used for decades.

What matrix factorization gives you is the geometry — the four corner clusters and which books sit near which. The names you read on the axes are a human convention layered on top. Swap the axes, flip a sign, and the picture turns or mirrors — but Kant still sits next to Smith, and Potter still sits next to Christie.
