The cost of distinguishability is quadratic in 1/Δ

When the audience's statistician says "we need a million users to A/B-test this," she means: the gap is small. Halve Δ and the sample count quadruples; shrink it tenfold and the cost goes up a hundredfold.

Lai & Robbins (1985) proved this is unavoidable for any algorithm — clever or not. The Shannon-flavored quantity sitting behind the bound is KL divergence: distributions that are close in KL are expensive to tell apart, no matter how you sample. The next slide makes that precise.
