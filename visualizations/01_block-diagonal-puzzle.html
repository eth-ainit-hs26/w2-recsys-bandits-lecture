Block-diagonal puzzle

21 readers rated 16 books, shown in a scrambled order. Rearrange the table until readers with the same taste sit together. Their ✅ ratings then form solid squares along the diagonal: that pattern is called block-diagonal.

How to play

  1. Move a reader: drag their name up or down.
  2. Move a book: drag its title left or right.
  3. Put readers who like the same books next to each other, and books liked by the same readers next to each other.
  4. Keep going until the score passes the line at 85%.

What's happening?

Each row is a reader, each column a book, each cell a rating. Scrambled, the table looks like noise. Sorted, it shows four kinds of reader (Commuters, Literary, Practical, Scholars) and four kinds of book (light and demanding fiction, light and demanding nonfiction). The ✅ squares sit where a reader group meets the books it likes.

Once you see the blocks, 21 readers boil down to four tastes. That shorter description is compression, and it is the grouping a recommender finds on its own, without anyone dragging rows. A few readers fit no group cleanly; keep an eye on them, the lecture comes back to them.
