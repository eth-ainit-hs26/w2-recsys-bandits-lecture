    Why this works

    The Eckart-Young-Mirsky theorem (1936) makes a strong promise: among all matrices of rank ≤ k, the truncated SVD Ak = Σr=1..k σr ur vr⊤ minimises ‖A − B‖F. No other rank-k approximation can do better in squared error — and the leftover error is exactly √(σk+1² + σk+2² + …).

    So when the curve above flattens after k = 2, it is not bad luck: it means σ3, σ4, … are tiny. Two latent factors really are enough.

    Eckart, C. & Young, G. (1936). The approximation of one matrix by another of lower rank. Psychometrika, 1, 211–218.
