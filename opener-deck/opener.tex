% ===== Frame 3. Compared to what =====
% Say: Now think as a manager. 35 percent of Amazon's purchases came through a recommendation. How
% many of those would have happened anyway? Kumar and Hosanagar ran a randomized experiment on the
% website of an apparel retailer: with recommendation links, the sales of a product and of its
% recommended substitutes were 11 percent higher than without them. Jannach and Jugovac reviewed the
% published field tests: 1 to 5 percent more revenue is the more usual report, and for companies of
% that size even 1 percent is a great deal of money. Ask every vendor: compared to what? That
% question runs through the experiments and the bandits of this weekend.

% ===== Frame 4. Six invented customers =====
% Say: An invented example. You have one retention offer and time for two calls. A churn model lists

% ===== Frame 5. The field studies behind the toy =====
% Say: The toy has a real counterpart. Eva Ascarza analysed two field experiments: at a membership
% organization in North America, which added a gift to its renewal communication in an A/B test,
% and at a wireless telecom firm in the Middle East, which texted an offer of extra credit for
% recharging. In simulations on both sets of data, aiming the offer at the customers it moves
% lowered churn by 4.1 to 8.7 percentage points more than aiming it at the customers most likely to
% leave. In some cases, aiming at the customers most likely to leave raised churn. A customer profile
% has to say what moves a customer, as well as what the customer is likely to do.
% Note for the lecturer: the 4.1 to 8.7 points are the American Marketing Association's summary,
% from the award it gave the paper in 2023. The paper's abstract gives no number, and an earlier
% talk abstract of hers said "up to seven percentage points". The paper body was not read.
\begin{frame}{Call the customers the offer can move}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=1cm]
    \path[use as bounding box] (0,0.1) rectangle (13,-5.4);
    \node[chip, align=flush center, text width=2.5cm, font=\normalsize] (offer) at (1.35,-2.2)
      {One retention offer};
    \node[chip strong, align=flush center, text width=3.2cm, font=\normalsize, inner xsep=2.6mm,
      inner ysep=2.4mm] (move) at (5.6,-1.0) {Aim at the customers it moves};
    \node[chip dim, align=flush center, text width=3.2cm, font=\normalsize, inner xsep=2.6mm,

    \treelink{edge strong}{elab}{offer}{move}{}

\end{frame}
