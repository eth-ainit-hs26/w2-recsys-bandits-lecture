% ===== Frame 3. Compared to what =====
% Say: Now think as a manager. 35 percent of Amazon's purchases came through a recommendation. How
% many of those would have happened anyway? Kumar and Hosanagar ran a randomized experiment on the
% website of an apparel retailer: with recommendation links, the sales of a product and of its
% recommended substitutes were 11 percent higher than without them. Jannach and Jugovac reviewed the
% published field tests: 1 to 5 percent more revenue is the more usual report, and for companies of
% that size even 1 percent is a great deal of money. Ask every vendor: compared to what? That
% question runs through the experiments and the bandits of this weekend.

% ===== Frame 4. What a better model adds: Meta, 2026 =====
% Say: The newest numbers, from Meta's earnings call of 21 October 2026, about July to September.
% Its finance chief Susan Li listed what each change to a model added. A conversion is what the
% advertiser wants from an ad: a purchase, an install, a sign-up. The large ranking model GEM,
% extended to more places, raised conversions by 9.6 percent for ads for apps, and by 1.7 percent
% for ads whose conversion happens on the advertiser's own website. A new generative way of picking
% the candidate ads raised them by nearly 2 percent on Facebook Feed, in the groups it targeted.
% Improvements to the retrieval model Andromeda, a more complex model among them, added 1.4 percent
% on Facebook. Note the sizes: a few percent per change. Advertisers notice: the average price of an
% ad rose 14 percent in a year, which Meta puts down to better ad performance and a better economy.

% ===== Frame 6. A customer profile learned from the raw transactions: Nubank, 2026 =====
% Say: Last, the customer profile itself. Nubank, a digital bank, describes how it reads its
% customers in a paper presented at a KDD workshop in 2026, whose latest version is of October 2026.
% Its credit model used 291 prepared features, such as the average spend over a period or a credit
% bureau score. Its new model reads the raw transactions, over 100 billion of them from over 100
% million customers, and learns a profile of each customer by itself. Added to the existing models,
% the learned profile improved every task the bank reports, each on its own score: income by 7.54
% percent, spending by 6.85, lending risk by 2.23, and credit default by 1.25 percent (by 1.81 in a
% second market). The credit model is in use for the credit decisions of over 100 million customers,
% and the bank says its gain matched a typical year of improvements. No new data went in: only a
% better reading of the transactions the bank already had. These are the bank's own figures,
% measured on its past data. How a machine learns such a profile is this weekend's subject, and we
% start with a video store.
