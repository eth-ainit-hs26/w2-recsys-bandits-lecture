% ===== 1. Adam Smith =====
% Say: The Wealth of Nations, 1776, opens with the division of labour, and its example is a pin factory. Making a pin
% takes about eighteen distinct operations. Smith saw a small manufactory where ten men made upwards of forty-eight
% thousand pins a day, four thousand eight hundred each; working alone, "they certainly could not each of them have
% made twenty, perhaps not one pin in a day". Same people, same tools: only the organisation changed. In 1820 and
% 1824 Prony wrote that he found Smith's book in a bookshop, opened it at the first chapter and conceived the plan
% of his tables (Roegel 2011, note 74).
% The picture: the wire drawer of the same Encyclopedie article, plate I.

% ===== 2. The task =====
% Say: The law of 1 December 1790 replaces the old taxes with a single tax on land. To set it fairly, France needs
% a cadastre: every parcel measured, its area computed. Prony directs the Bureau du cadastre from 5 October 1791.
% Surveyors measure angles quickly and lengths slowly, so they measure land in triangles: one side and two angles,
% and the rest is computed with tables of logarithms. The new instruments count angles in the new decimal division
% of the circle, so the old tables no longer serve. At the end of 1793 Carnot and Prieur, of the Committee of Public
% Safety, and Brunet order new tables from Prony, "the greatest and most imposing monument of computation ever made,
% or even conceived" (Prony, letter to Arago): the sines, the logarithms of the sines and tangents, and the
% logarithms of the numbers up to 200,000.
% The picture: the principal triangles of the survey of France around Paris, from Rouen to Reims.

% ===== 3. The three tiers =====
% Say: Prony divides the work into three tiers. The mathematicians (Legendre among them) choose formulas that many
% people can work through at the same time. The analysts turn the formulas into the first values of each page and
% check the pages that come back. The computers fill in each page by additions and subtractions alone, 900 to
% 1,000 of them a day. A computer was a person: the word named a job. In 1824 Prony describes sheets of 100 lines:
% the analysts compute the first line, the computers the other 99. Prony gave sixty or eighty computers in 1801 and
% seventy or eighty in 1824; the payrolls show about twenty (Roegel 2011). Babbage (1832, section 244): those who
% knew only the first two rules of arithmetic were usually the most correct. The second and third tiers each worked
% in two sections that did the same work independently, one checking the other. The work began at the end of 1793
% and was probably done by mid-1796, at the Palais Bourbon. The photograph is a later computing room: Harvard,
% about 1890.
\begin{frame}[plain,noframenumbering]
