Two Engines

Memory, prediction, and the difference between them.

A 90-minute lecture on recommendation systems and bandits, told around a single question. Compression — the shortest faithful description of the past — and prediction — the best bet about the future — are two views of the same subject, governed by Shannon's 1948 theorems. We build them in two halves and collapse them in the third.

Carlos Cotrini · ETH Zürich · Spring 2026

Visualizations

Eighteen single-file interactive HTML pieces, each opens directly in a modern browser (no server). The ten anchored to the lecture's twenty-seven scenes are listed first, grouped by act; eight bonus pieces from the earlier draft of the recsys deck sit at the bottom.

Act I — Compression (recsys)

Scene 5
Block-diagonal puzzle
Drag rows and columns until the four-quadrant clustering reveals itself. Compression by hand.
Scene 7
Latent-axes lab
Sixteen books in a 2-D plane. Swap and flip the axes — the geometry is invariant; the names are not.
Scene 9
Spectral microscope
SVD as a slider. Watch the matrix sharpen as k grows from 1; the elbow at k = 2 is Eckart-Young.
Scene 11 — anchor
Customer-urns carousel
Twenty-one urns; click to draw, then compress to four archetype urns. Stage 3 introduces the cold-start reader Vera.
Scene 12
Embeddings that learn
Random embeddings under gradient descent organise themselves into the four-corner geometry. Re-seed and watch it again.

Interlude — Shannon

Scene 14
Entropy mountain
Drag the bias of an urn from 0 to 1 and trace the bell curve H(p). The compression floor, made physical.

Act II — Prediction (bandits)

Scene 20
Three-urn casino
Three campaigns, three hidden conversion rates. Play 30 rounds, then watch ε-greedy and naive replay your seed.
Scene 22
Algorithm stadium
Same urns, same rounds. Five strategies racing on cumulative regret — and a slider for the explore parameter.
Scene 23
Distinguishability dial
Two urns, slider for the gap. The required sample size explodes quadratically as the urns approach each other.

Epilogue — Duality

Scene 25 — climax
Compression-prediction twin
Forecaster on the left, arithmetic encoder on the right. Three modes: binary urn, English text, character-level Markov LM. Loss ≡ rate, bit-exact.

Bonus pieces (from the earlier recsys draft)

These eight viz predate the v3 narrative plan and are not on the path the lecture walks. They explore single ideas — the inner product, the dot-product computation, a single rating, an outer-product painter, matrix completion, the MovieLens taste map, embedding interpolation, and a cold-start cliff curve — and remain useful for self-study.

Bonus
Inner-product compass
Drag a taste vector around; half the plane glows green, the other half red. The dot product as a hemisphere.
Bonus
Dot-product bistro
Bars for the user, bars for the item, products in between, one number at the bottom. The whole formula breathing.
Bonus
Build a single rating
Click a cell. Its row and column light up, products fly out, the answer lands. "Compute all" cascades the matrix.
Bonus
Outer-product painters
Add rank-1 layers one at a time. Same trick compresses an image — proof on the second tab.
Bonus
Filling the holes
Predict the unseen cells. Watch validation accuracy climb as the model trains.
Bonus
MovieLens taste map
Fifty films in a 2-D taste plane. Drop a "you" vector and watch the top-10 recommendations morph as you drag.
Bonus
Embedding interpolation
Slide between two readers. Their predicted preferences morph cell-by-cell along the way.
Bonus
Cold-start cliff
Slide N from 0 to 16 ratings for a new reader. Prediction quality climbs sharply — and plateaus.
