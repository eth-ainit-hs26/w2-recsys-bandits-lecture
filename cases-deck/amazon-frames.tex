% ===== Frame 1. Which page for which visitor =====
% Say: Amazon Payments invites customers to apply for one of its products; the post does not say which. The page
% shows one image and one tagline. Amazon builds the pairs from a small set of reviewed parts, images about
% industries and taglines about benefits, so there are many pairs, and each pair is an arm of a bandit. An
% application has three steps: started, submitted, approved. Approvals are rare, and they come days later.
% [click] And the steps pull against each other. A page that wins many starts draws a broad audience, but approval
% depends on whether the offer suits the applicant. Amazon calls it the seesaw problem: optimizing one step alone
% can hurt another.
\begin{frame}{Amazon Payments: which page for which visitor?}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1mm, y=1mm]
    \path[use as bounding box] (0,1) rectangle (130,-54);
    \node[body, anchor=north west, text width=64mm] at (0,0)

       many possible pairs.};
    \node[body, anchor=north west, text width=64mm, visible on=<2->] at (0,-22)
      {A page that wins the first step can lose the last.};
    % the steps of one application, narrowing as a funnel
    \node[lbl, anchor=north] at (99.5,0.4) {An application};
    \node[stage, minimum width=54mm] (s1) at (99.5,-9.5) {Started};
    \node[stage, minimum width=42mm] (s2) at (99.5,-22.5) {Submitted};
    \node[stage, minimum width=30mm] (s3) at (99.5,-35.5) {Approved};
    \draw[flow] (s1) to (s2);
    \draw[flow] (s2) to node[elab, inner sep=0.4pt, anchor=west, xshift=1.2mm] {days later} (s3);
    \node[src, anchor=north west, text width=130mm] at (0,-50.6) {\amsource};
  \end{tikzpicture}
\end{frame}

% ===== Frame 2. One UCB score for each step =====
% Say: How the bandit picks a pair, in the words of our mirror game. In the mirror, UCB scored each product by its
% estimate plus a bonus: the fewer dots, the bigger the bonus. Amazon does the same for every pair, with one
% difference: the estimate and the bonus depend on the visitor, on what Amazon knows of their payments. The bonus
% is large for a kind of visitor the pair has rarely seen. And there is one score for each step: started,
% submitted, approved. These numbers are invented. A bandit for the first step alone would choose pair A: the best
% score for starts, but the worst for approvals. [click] Amazon adds the three scores, with about equal weights, and
% shows the pair with the highest sum: pair B. Before the launch, Amazon's offline estimates showed that a bandit
% for any single step consistently pushed at least one other step down. The scores are computed once a week for every prospect,
% and the page only looks up the chosen pair; a visitor without one sees the fixed page.
\begin{frame}{One UCB score for each step}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1mm, y=1mm]
    \path[use as bounding box] (0,1) rectangle (130,-54);
    \node[body, anchor=north west, text width=130mm] at (0,0)
      {Each score is an estimate plus a bonus, as in the mirror.};
    % the table, INVENTED scores (tools/cases_numbers.py, AM_PAIRS): cells 18 mm wide that touch
    \node[lbl, anchor=base west] at (0,-13) {For one visitor};
    \foreach \cx/\ctext in {44/Started, 62/Submitted, 80/Approved} {\node[lbl, anchor=base] at (\cx,-13) {\ctext};}
    \node[lbl, anchor=base, visible on=<2->] at (104,-13) {Sum};
    \foreach \ry/\rp in {-18/A, -23.6/B, -29.2/C} {\node[body, anchor=west] at (0,\ry) {Pair \rp};}

    \scell{1-}{62}{-29.2}{\amCsub}
    \scell{1-}{80}{-29.2}{\amCapp}

    \scellwin{2-}{104}{-23.6}{\amBsum}

      {Amazon shows the highest sum: pair B.};
    \node[src, anchor=north west, text width=130mm] at (0,-50.6) {Invented scores. \amsource};
  \end{tikzpicture}
\end{frame}

% ===== Frame 3. The A/B test =====
% Say: The value. Amazon ran an A/B test of seven weeks: the bandit against the fixed page, in two groups of
% customers. In the first group all three steps went up so far, approvals by a high single-digit percentage, a
% relative change. The post gives no exact figure, and it calls these lifts directional: it does not say they are
% statistically significant. [click] In the second group the bandit explored most of its pairs and found none that
% beat the fixed page. Approvals fell, and the drop was statistically significant. [click] Amazon's reading: the model did its job. The pool of images and taglines held
% no winner for that group, and the exploring showed it. Amazon's advice for such a case: renew the images and taglines and keep the algorithm. Amazon also
% advises making the fixed page one of the arms, so that UCB falls back on it when nothing beats it.
\begin{frame}{The value: an A/B test of \amWeeks{} weeks}
