% ===== Frame 1. Etsy, the shortlist, and the words that do not match =====
% Say: Etsy is the online marketplace for handmade, vintage and craft items, more than 100 million of them. For
% each search it first picks a shortlist of several thousand items, and a ranking model then orders the
% shortlist. The shortlist used to come from keyword matching only: an item got in when its title or tags shared
% words with the search. That fails when buyers and sellers use different words. [click] A buyer types "mothers
% day gift"; the seller of a birth flower necklace never wrote the word gift. Etsy's paper names exactly such
% searches as common on Etsy and hard to serve from the words alone. The listing here is made up.

% ===== Frame 3. A list of numbers for every search and every item =====
% Say: From those rows Etsy trains two networks. The first reads the search together with the person: the
% words, the location, the recent searches, the shops clicked. Out comes a list of numbers. The second reads an
% item: its title, tags and category, and the searches that led to it in the past. Out comes a list of numbers
% of the same length. Here four numbers each, made up; Etsy's lists are longer. [click] Training moves the
% numbers until the inner product, multiply number by number and add, as in the video store, is high for the
% item someone bought after that search and low for random items. For our search and the necklace it is 7.
\begin{frame}{Every search and every item becomes a list of numbers}

      {The search words, the location,\\ recent searches and shops clicked};
    \node[anchor=north, inner sep=0, font=\small, text width=58mm, align=center] at (100,0)
      {The item's title, tags and category,\\ and the searches that led to it before};
    \node[net] (qnet) at (30,-17.5) {Search network};

% ===== Frame 4. The new search: sort by inner product =====
% Say: Now a search arrives. [the frame opens on it] The search network turns "mothers day gift", with this
% person's context, into its list of numbers. [click] The item numbers were computed in advance, one list per
% item; here five items. [click] For each item, the inner product with the search: multiply number by number,
% then add. For the necklace: 4, 2, 1 and 0, sum 7. [click] The same for every item. [click] Sort by the inner
% product: the top of the list joins the shortlist. The necklace comes first although it shares no word with
% the search, and the gift wrap, which has the word gift, falls out. Etsy does this for more than 100 million

% ===== Frame 5. What it cost and what it paid =====

% easy? No. Measure: of the products people bought after a popular search, how many were among a version's top
% 100? A plain version that read only the search words and the item text found 32 percent; keyword search found
